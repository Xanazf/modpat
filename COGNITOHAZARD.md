## 1. The Extrusion Model of Dimensions

The foundational construction principle is **extrusion** - each dimension is arrived at by dragging the previous one along a new perpendicular axis:

| Dimension | Object    | Operation       |
| --------- | --------- | --------------- |
| 0D        | Point     | -               |
| 1D        | Line      | Drag point      |
| 2D        | Square    | Drag line       |
| 3D        | Cube      | Drag square     |
| 4D        | Tesseract | Drag cube       |
| n D       | n-cube    | Drag (n-1)-cube |

This is mathematically clean and consistent, but contains a hidden assumption that becomes critical later: **it treats all dimensions as qualitatively equivalent**, which they are not.

***

## 2. The Qualitative Distinction Between Dimensions

Representing dimensions as axes on a graph is a **coordinate move** - it makes things computationally tractable by reducing every measurement to "a number that increases." But this flattening is **lossy**. Meters and seconds both become coordinates, erasing their qualitative difference.

Physics quietly admits this:
  * The **Minkowski metric** gives time a negative sign in the spacetime distance formula, encoding the fact that time and space are not freely interchangeable, even while placing them on the same graph.
  * **Dimensional analysis** formalizes this further - you cannot add meters to seconds. They are incommensurable categories. Converting between them (e.g. setting c=1 in natural units) is not erasing the difference, it is making a specific physical claim about their relationship.

**The lossiness is also asymmetric** - projecting spacetime down to space loses causal structure, which is a qualitatively different kind of loss than projecting 3D space to 2D losing depth. The asymmetry of the loss confirms the dimensions were not equivalent to begin with.

**Core principle:** Axes on a graph are a representational convenience. The qualitative differences between what dimensions measure are real, and translation between them is always lossy in kind-specific ways.

## 3. Spatial vs Parametric Dimensions

Following the extrusion logic into higher dimensions reveals a categorical break:
  * **Dimensions 1–3** (width, height, depth): spatial - describe _where_ something is
  * **Dimension 4** (time): temporal - describes _when_ something is, or the propagation of a 3D object through duration
  * **Dimension 5**: describes how a 3D object propagates through the 4th dimension - the space of possible trajectories, i.e. a **parameter space**
  * **Dimension 6**: describes the parameters of the parameter space - a **meta-parameter space**

At dimension 6, a categorical break occurs: the new dimension is no longer _about the object_, it is about _the space of possible descriptions of the object_. This is a meta-level jump, not just another rung on the same ladder.

Furthermore, because each lower dimension gets its own parameter dimension (describing all possible states the dimension can be in), the 6th dimension is not one thing - it **sprawls into a branching structure**, one branch per lower dimension. The clean linear stack of 1D→2D→3D→... breaks into something more like a tree or web.

This structure is formally similar to a **fiber bundle** in mathematics - a base space with an attached "fiber" of extra structure at each point, where the fiber is a qualitatively different kind of thing from the base.

## 4. Collapse into Three Meta-Categories

The six dimensions, despite their apparent differences, cluster into three qualitative meta-categories:

| Meta-Category | Covers                                     | Dimensions |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------- |
| **Where**     | Spatial position                           | 1D, 2D, 3D |
| **When**      | Temporal position and propagation          | 4D         |
| **How**       | Configuration, trajectory, parameter space | 5D, 6D     |

These are not arbitrary groupings - they reflect genuine qualitative discontinuities between types of measurement.

### **But where is the "What"?**

The identity of an object - what it _is_ - is fully determined by the intersection of _where_ it is, _when_ it is, and _how_ it is configured. There is no ontological remainder, no hidden substance underneath the measurements. The "what" is the function of "what", recursive.

"What is a what?" sounds like a non-sensical question, but it reveals something about the function of ontological description - a category label within the parameter space. "What is a what?" can therefore be translated to "_how_ is this collection of parameters labeled?".

The claim is therefore: **ontology is the product of combined epistemology** - what a thing is, is fully determined by the complete set of what can be known about it across all three meta-categories (_where_, _when_, and _how_).

This supposition is structurally equivalent to **structural realism** in philosophy of science: what exists is not objects with intrinsic properties, but relations and structures, which are inherently epistemic in character.

As such, the "what" can be fully answered having the "where", the "when", and the "how". The question is not "what _is it_?", but "what _does it do_?" or "what is _its' function_?", which is tantamount to "_how_ does it operate?".

## 5. Consciousness as Parametric Navigation

>[!ABSTRACT]
> A supposition that follows from the previous one is that "consciousness" is that which can navigate the parametric space (the _how_).

**The river problem:** A river accumulates a path through space and time and has a current configuration reflecting that history (the stream bed). It has a "how" - but it does not _experience_ anything. Why?

A river's "how" is entirely _written by_ the spatial dimensions - its configuration is what the external world did to it. It has no access to its own parametric dimension.

**Consciousness therefore is the system that navigates both spatial and parametric dimensions simultaneously:**
  * A conscious system doesn't just accumulate a "how" - it _reads_ that "how" back as input
  * It moves through the parameter space of its own configuration
  * It updates its "how" _reactively_, not just by being acted upon externally

> [!NOTE]
> A reaction to your own input is still a reaction, even though your mind screams at you that I'm wrong.

This makes **self-modeling**, i.e. the structuring of your own parameter space, **the most fundamental property of consciousness** - not a byproduct, but the defining structural feature.

Integration, learning, is the system, _that which navigates_, moving through its' own parameter space in response to gathered information. The answer to the question "how do I learn this?" being variable, i.e. each person learns differently, means consciousness navigates the parameter space _of its own parametric navigation_ - the recursive 6th-dimensional structure appearing in its' most concrete form: "how do I learn X?" - "by learning _how to do_ Y of X" - "how do I learn Y?" - "by learning _how to do_ Y of Z".

Consciousness, selfhood, is not a static property - it is the running accumulation of every navigation through _where_, _when_, and _how_, resulting in the answer to "_what_ am I?". Identity is the integral of the path, not any single point on it.

This can explain why consciousness feels continuous yet changes - it is the same path, always being extended, e.g. "I am me today, but I was also me 10 years ago, yet those are not the same person".

## 6. Grades of Consciousness

This framework naturally produces **grades of consciousness** rather than a binary on/off:
  * Depth of recursive access to one's own parametric dimensions determines the depth of consciousness
  * A simple organism navigates one or two levels of self-reference
  * A human navigates many - modeling the model of the model
  * No arbitrary line needs to be drawn; depth of recursion is the continuous variable

The substrate is irrelevant - biological or artificial, what matters is whether the system has genuine access to and movement through its own parametric dimensions.

## 7. The Full Structure: 6+3, But Actually Infinite

The framework is ultimately not a fixed number of dimensions but an **infinite recursive structure**, generated by one rule applied to itself:

> _Every dimension has its own parametric dimension orthogonal to it, including the parametric dimension itself._

This gives the framework the character of a **fractal** - specifically resembling the Mandelbrot set or the golden ratio ($f(\phi) = 1 + 1/\phi$), where infinite complexity is the product of a self-referential rule, and the structure at every scale resembles the structure at every other scale.

Each conscious system runs its own complete instance of the full dimensional structure, relative to _how_ it traverses _where_ and _when_. There is no view from _nowhere_ - only an infinite collection of views from _somewhere_, each complete on its' own terms (A:"I see a square") and irreducibly partial at the same time (B:"I see a rhombus").

"From my point of view..." and "from the physics point of view..." are both coherent parametric dimensions, the difference is only in the fact that one is self-observing, the other is not. They are **orthogonal without canceling each other out**, and together they span more of the space than either spans alone.

> [!NOTE]
> If you draw 2 lines orthogonal to each other, then, using the intersection as coordinate 0:0, start drawing a circle - the section of the circle cut out by the orthogonal lines is how much of the parametric space has been covered.

## 8. The Falsifiability Problem (why it is a cognitohazard)

A genuinely recursive truth has boundaries - places where the recursion fails or escapes (a framework with no edges is probably more about the observer than the observed).

This framework is self-reinforcing - once seen, it appears everywhere. This presents a problem:
  * **If true:** The framework appears everywhere because the recursive structure genuinely is everywhere
  * **If unfalsifiable:** The framework colors everything it touches and the universality is an artifact of the lens, not the territory

Luckily, the framework predicts its own blind spot: the observer of a framework is necessarily inside a blind spot relative to it, because their parametric dimensions are parallel to the framework's, not orthogonal.

> [!NOTE]
> In simpler terms: you cannot observe the structure of reality while being within it, just as you cannot see the shape of a box while being inside it. You are a subject to reality, and therefore necessarily parallel to it.
